FOR DATA STEWARDS
NMWC Customer Master · v1.0 · 2026-05

Welcome

You own the integrity of the customer master from head office. Your work is heavier than the field roles — you bulk-import lists from the ERP, merge duplicates that the field can't see across regions, monitor the entire audit trail, and export the cleaned master back to downstream systems. This guide covers every screen you will use, with a focus on the safety rules: every steward action is high-trust, immediately visible across the company, and logged forever.

1. Logging in and your scope

Your account sees everything: every region, every route, every customer. Other roles cannot.

  1. Open https://nmwc-cm.vercel.app on your laptop. The steward workflow is desktop-first because of the bulk-import / export / duplicate-review screens.
  2. Sign in with your steward credentials.
  3. After login you land on the Import page by default. The left sidebar shows your full menu: Import, Export, Customers, Duplicates, Work items.
Your power and your responsibilityYou can edit, soft-delete, and merge any customer in any region. There is no four-eyes approval on most steward actions — the audit log is your accountability. Always cross-check before any bulk action.

2. Browse the customer master

See the full master across all regions and routes.

  1. Tap Customers in the sidebar.
  2. You see every customer in every region. Filter by region, channel, payment terms, or completeness score.
  3. The search box runs on legal name, NMWC code, or primary phone — partial matches work (e.g. "lulu" finds every Lulu branch).
  4. Click any row to open the customer profile and see the full record + branches + edit history + audit trail.

3. Import — bulk upload from xlsx

Two distinct importers, one entry point, and a fixed order. The templates for both files are in docs/import-templates (account-master-template.xlsx, customer-master-template.xlsx) with an Instructions tab each.

  1. Before any import: make sure the Manager and Steward accounts already exist in Users. The import deliberately cannot create or promote those two roles — no administrator can ever be minted from a spreadsheet. Managers must exist first because the account master then assigns them their regions.
  2. Tap Import in the sidebar.
  3. Account Master (left card) goes first: a workbook with three sheets — Regions, Routes, Users. Existing rows with matching keys are updated in place; new rows are added; an existing user keeps their password, role and supervisor unless a column says otherwise. Routes must exist before the customers that reference them.
  4. Customer Master (right card) goes second: a single-sheet workbook, one row per branch. Region and route are matched on their codes, never their names. It goes to a staged batch first — nothing changes in the live master until you review and promote.
  5. Both cards have an Expected columns expandable — read it once before your first import to confirm header names match.
  6. Click Choose File, pick the .xlsx, then click Upload. Wait for the upload to finish.
Recommended monthly cadenceImport account master changes (new salesman, retired route) within 24h of HR notifying you. Import customer master refreshes from the ERP at month-end after the ERP team confirms their export is final.
Account master writes immediatelyUnlike the customer master, the account-master path applies its changes the moment the upload completes (after Zod validation). There is no staging step. Double-check the workbook before clicking Upload.

4. Review, promote and reconcile a staged customer batch

A customer-master import is staged, promoted in passes, and then reconciled. It is not finished when the screen stops moving — it is finished when the six figures add up.

  1. After the customer-master upload finishes, you land on the batch page. The Recent batches table on the Import page also lists every batch.
  2. The page shows six figures: Total · Clean · Quarantined · Promoted · Rejected · Left to promote. Rows that need a decision (REJECTED and QUARANTINED) are always listed first in the table below, each with its reason, so none can hide further down a long file.
  3. Review the QUARANTINED rows first — bad phone format, an unknown channel or visit-day code, a duplicate phone or CR on a different customer. Fix the source row in your copy of the xlsx and re-import it, or accept that it stays out. Quarantined rows are never promoted.
  4. Click Promote N clean rows. On a full master this runs in passes — the button reads “Promoting… 1,200 done, 2,100 left” and keeps going by itself. Leave the tab open until it reports Done.
  5. If it is interrupted — tab closed, connection dropped — nothing is lost. Everything already promoted is saved. The page shows Promote interrupted with a Resume promote button that continues exactly where it stopped, and the batch appears in your Work list as “Import to resume” so it cannot be forgotten.
  6. Only one customer import can be promoted at a time. A second attempt is refused and names the file already running — finish (or abandon) that one first.
  7. When it finishes, reconcile: Left to promote must be 0, and Promoted + Rejected + Quarantined must equal Total. Then open every REJECTED row: a rejected row is not in the master. Typical reasons are a branch code that already belongs to another customer, or a Temix code recorded on a different customer. Correct the source and re-import those customers.
  8. Record the six figures (a screenshot of the batch page is enough) as the evidence that the load was checked. The load is complete only when every rejected and quarantined row is resolved or formally accepted as excluded.
Why staging, and why passes?A bad bulk-import that lands directly into the master is hard to undo, so the batch is staged and you see exactly what would change before committing. A real master is thousands of rows — far too many for one request — so promotion works through it in passes, each pass committing its customers and recording an audit row. No customer is ever loaded twice and none is skipped, however many times the load is resumed.
A technical fault is not a rejectionIf the database falters mid-pass, the affected customers are not rejected — they are retried on the next pass. If a pass makes no progress at all the load stops and tells you; escalate that to IT rather than clicking Resume repeatedly.
Imported customers skip the approval chainCustomers loaded this way are created directly — they do not go through Supervisor → Finance → GM → Accountant. That is deliberate (they are existing customers being migrated) and it is why importing is yours alone and fully audited. Every customer created in the field after go-live follows the chain in full.

5. Find and merge duplicates

Duplicates are the master's biggest enemy. The steward's queue surfaces three kinds.

  1. Tap Duplicates in the sidebar.
  2. Each pair card shows the match reason at the top — PHONE (exact phone number match across two customers), CR (same Commercial Registration number), or NAME (fuzzy similarity ≥ 0.7).
  3. Compare the two cards side-by-side: NMWC code, phone, CR, branch count. The customer with more branches and a higher completeness score is usually the better keeper.
  4. Three actions:
    • Mark distinct — these are NOT duplicates. The pair is recorded in the audit log and will not surface again.
    • Keep ← — the LEFT customer is the survivor. The right one is soft-deleted. Its branches and edit history move to the survivor.
    • Keep → — same, but the RIGHT customer survives.
Cross-region merges need a reasonIf the two customers are in different regions, the merge requires you to confirm explicitly and write a 5+ character reason. The reason ends up in the audit trail.
Merges are permanentA merge soft-deletes one customer. It can be reversed only by a steward intervention via the audit log + a manual restore. Be sure before you click Keep.

6. Export — the master for the ERP, and the field-update report

When the field team has enriched enough records, you push the cleaned master back downstream — and you can see exactly what they changed.

  1. Tap Export in the sidebar.
  2. Pick filters: region(s), route(s), status, payment terms (cash / credit), completeness threshold (e.g. only records ≥ 80% complete), updated since.
  3. Click Download .xlsx — one row per branch in the import shape, so it can be re-uploaded after editing. Download all (no filters) gives the whole master (up to 60,000 rows in one file).
  4. Field-update report: in the amber box pick the window (from / until) and click Download field-update report. Sheet Customers is the same master with every cell changed by an approved salesman edit in the window in yellow (hover for was → now, by, when), pending proposals in orange, photos added in yellow; sheet Changes lists every change, By salesman the totals, Legend the colours. Unhighlighted cells were not touched.
  5. If a download fails with "too large", narrow by region or route and try again.
A good completeness thresholdFor mid-pilot exports use 60% — gets you the rows with names, phones, GPS, and at least one photo. For year-end exports use 90% — gives you only the records the field has fully validated.

7. Audit log — your investigation tool

Every action by every user, immutable, queryable.

  1. Tap Audit log in the sidebar.
  2. Each row: who, when, from what IP and user-agent, what action (CREATE / UPDATE / APPROVE / REJECT / MERGE / IMPORT / REACTIVATE / LOGIN / LOGIN_FAIL / FORCE_OVERRIDE / DELETE / SOFT_DELETE / PHOTO_VIEW), on what entity, with old and new values stored as JSON.
  3. Filter by user (e.g. investigate one salesman), date range (e.g. last week), entity type (Customer / Branch / CustomerEdit / User), or action type.
  4. For an HR investigation: filter by user + date range + entity type Customer to see exactly what they changed.
  5. For a "what happened" investigation on one customer: open the customer, click History — same data filtered to that record.
Audit logs are immutableOnce written, no one — not even you — can edit or delete an audit row. By design. This is the trustworthy paper trail. Even FORCE_OVERRIDE actions by managers leave a row.

8. Routes, regions, and channel taxonomy

The fixed reference tables that everything else depends on.

  1. Tap Routes & regions in the sidebar.
  2. You see every region (e.g. Muscat) and every route under it (C1, C4, MH01, etc.).
  3. Add a new route when a new salesman starts. Mark a route inactive when it's consolidated. Don't delete — it would break audit trails for past customers.
  4. The Channel taxonomy (General Trade, HORECA, Modern Trade, sub-channels) is locked — see PRD Appendix A. If a sub-channel is missing, file an ops ticket; do not edit the taxonomy live.

9. User management — view-only for stewards

Stewards see all users for visibility but most user-edit actions belong to managers.

  1. Tap Users in the sidebar.
  2. You see users in every region. Useful for understanding "who reports to whom" and confirming the org chart matches what HR has.
  3. The list opens on the Active tab, which is NOT every account: the pilot and QA accounts kept for their audit history are deactivated, and they sit on Disabled. When you reconcile the roster against HR, read it from the All tab — otherwise the counts will not match and accounts will look missing.
  4. Reset password, disable, and reassign-route actions live with the user's manager. Forward HR requests to the right manager.
  5. You CAN onboard a new manager (since managers don't have a higher-rank approver) — head office hands you the request, you create the user record with the MANAGER role and the regions they cover.

10. Backup, recovery, and your responsibilities

You are not the database admin, but you are the first to know when something goes wrong.

ConcernWhat you do
Daily DB backup — check the GitHub Actions tab once a week to confirm the nightly run is green.If a run is red two days in a row, alert ops. The dump goes to the nmwc-backups R2 bucket as db/<DATE>.sql.gz.
Photo storage — R2 lifecycle deletes "gc-marked" photos after 7 days.You don't touch this. It runs on its own. If you suspect lost photos, check the audit log first.
"I deleted the wrong customer"Open the audit log, find the SOFT_DELETE row for that customer, get the JSON of the prior state, and restore it via a steward-only Prisma script (head-office ops can write this).
"I promoted a bad batch"Audit log filters action=IMPORT — find the batch row, then write a one-off undo script with ops. Don't try to revert via the UI.
Quarterly drillRun the manual restore-drill GitHub Action. Confirms the latest dump can actually be restored into a Neon branch.
Never run scripts against the production DB without opsStewards have full read access to everything. Direct write scripts to fix mistakes must be run by head-office ops, peer-reviewed, and committed to git. Never run an unsaved one-off SQL command on prod.

11. Common situations

SituationWhat to do
"Cannot promote — quarantined rows"Open the batch, fix or reject the QUARANTINED rows, then re-promote.
"Phone now belongs to X (Y) — reject and ask the salesman"A salesman submitted a phone that conflicts with another customer in the master. Reject the edit; the salesman fixes at the shop.
"Manager creates regions silently"A new region was added without the usual ticket. Check audit log for action=CREATE entityType=Region. If unauthorized, escalate.
"Customer master has many duplicates"Run a duplicate review session. Sort by similarity. Tackle PHONE matches first (1.0 similarity = same phone), then CR, then NAME.
"Export failed"Check the row's error message. Most are network blips — re-run. If it persists, check the date range — exports over 50k rows can time out.
"User left the company"Forward to their manager. The manager uses the Users page → Disable. Their audit trail stays.
