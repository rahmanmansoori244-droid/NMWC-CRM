# Data retention schedule

**Status: Accepted by the owner on 11 October 2026.** The periods are as written below, unchanged (among them: notifications 90 days once read and 180 days unread, import upload details 90 days, removed photos 30 days). The items marked **[OWNER]** or **[COUNSEL]** below are still open.

**Gap 25 (data audit 2026-10-10):** the periods below are unchanged. What changed is that every
clean-up the application runs now leaves a summary of what it deleted: the **Summary kept** column
names the rule id (`lib/retention-rules.ts`, the single source of the periods, held to this table by
`tests/unit/retention-rules-doc.test.ts`), and each batch writes one `PurgeSummary` row in the same
statement as the deletion. Deletions outside the application (the R2 lifecycle expiry, the dump
expiry, Neon PITR, Vercel and Sentry logs) cannot write one; the photo-gc summary and the photo's
`AttachmentHistory` DELETE row record the decision that leads to the R2 expiry.
Created 2026-09-14 (blocker B6).

Two columns matter more than the periods themselves: **Enforced by** and **Proven by**. Before today most of this schedule existed only as prose, and two of the four real rules were dashboard tasks nobody had confirmed. A retention period that nothing implements is not a retention period.

## Why the rate-limit period is one day and not thirty

The row exists solely to hold token-bucket state — a token count and a refill timestamp. The bucket refills within minutes. Everything after that is a record of which username was tried from which IP address, kept for no operational purpose. One day is already generous.

## What the sweep deliberately does not touch

`AuditLog` and `EditApproval`. They are retained on purpose, and any change to them is an owner-authorised maintenance operation under `SET LOCAL nmwc.audit_maintenance = 'on'` — never a scheduled job. See `docs/OPERATIONS.md` for the maintenance-window procedure and `PDPL-ASSESSMENT.md` Q4 for the erasure question this creates.

## Known gaps, stated rather than hidden

1. **Archiving a customer does not release its photographs.** Soft-deleting a customer and its branches sets `deletedAt` on those records only; `Attachment.deletedAt` is untouched, so the CR documents and shopfront images of an archived customer are never garbage-collected. Fixing this means deciding whether an archived customer's evidence should survive the archive — a business question, not a cleanup bug.
2. **Nothing records what a maintenance window was used for.** By construction: the audit of a ledger edit would live in the ledger being edited. The mitigation is procedural — see the maintenance-window procedure in `docs/OPERATIONS.md`. Since the watch-and-prove branch the EFFECT is detected: a sealed ledger row changed, deleted or added in a window breaks the nightly seal chain (`seal-chain-intact`, `scripts/ops/verify-seals.ts`), but the seal says that a row changed, not who changed it or why.
3. **Backups defeat erasure for up to 30 days**, and Neon PITR for 7. The standard industry answer is a written backup exception; whether that answer is available under Omani law is **[COUNSEL]** (`PDPL-ASSESSMENT.md` Q5), not something this document should assume.
4. **`SavedView.urlParams` and `CronHeartbeat.lastDetail`** have no retention. Both are small; both can embed a searched name or phone. Listed so they are not forgotten.
5. **Photographs taken for a change request that is never finished are kept forever.** A net-new CREATE uploads its images against `Attachment.editId` before the customer exists, and `services/creates.ts` releases those claimed images only when the *same* edit row is re-submitted. A request left in `DRAFT` or `NEEDS_CORRECTION` therefore keeps its photographs indefinitely — including the commercial registration of a customer that was never created, and a shopfront that may show bystanders. No scheduled job covers this, and adding one needs a period from the owner first: "abandoned" is a judgement about how long a salesman may reasonably leave a correction outstanding, not a technical default.

6. **A held-back import row that is never fixed or excluded keeps its payload indefinitely** — names, phones, CR numbers, addresses. The Work list keeps such a batch in view until every row is fixed or excluded (item 20). A row fixed in the app and waiting in a batch that is never promoted again is not swept either (promote still needs it); the batch's promoted and rejected rows are. Account-master issue rows have no in-app fix at all.
7. **Vercel's runtime logs hold some personal data unscrubbed for 30 days** (since the Pro upgrade and Observability Plus, 2026-09-27; it was 1 hour on Hobby). Vercel records every request's path with its query string, and the customer search carries what was typed, often a phone number or a name, in `?q=`. Next also prints an unhandled server error's message and stack itself, outside the app's scrubbing logger. The app's own log lines and Prisma's error lines are scrubbed. Options, none taken yet: send the search as a POST body instead of a query string; shorten the retention by turning Observability Plus off (back to 1 day, which loses item 10's log search); or accept 30 days and record it in the processor register (Vercel, P1).

8. **Notification e-mail leaves copies the system cannot retain or erase** (F1, 2026-10-05). Every message sent is kept in the sending Gmail account's Sent folder and in its recipient's mailbox, at whatever provider runs it. The content is deliberately minimal (counts, the kind of each request, links; `lib/email/digest.ts` never reads a notification's title or body, which carry the customer's name), so what those copies disclose is who was told of how many requests and when. Options, none taken yet: a Gmail filter or a periodic clean-up of the Sent folder (the owner's mailbox, so the owner's call); and telling recipients (PDPL-ASSESSMENT.md Q3). Nothing is sent until the owner sets `NOTIFY_EMAIL_ENABLED=on`.

**Settled by the merge fix, so the two are not confused:** merging two duplicate customers *re-parents* the loser's documents to the winner and releases nothing. A merge asserts the two rows are the same legal entity, so the evidence is the surviving customer's. Only gap 1 — the archive — is still an open policy question.
